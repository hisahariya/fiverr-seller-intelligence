How our data works

Every keyword, tag and price on this site comes from somewhere. Here is exactly where, how far you can trust it today, and how it gets more accurate over time.

The short version

Today

Guidance

Every subcategory starts with suggested starting points written by our team, based on how gigs in that category are usually packaged and priced. A sensible place to start, not market data.

Next

Checked by hand

Each month a person compares a few categories with live Fiverr gigs in a normal browser, the way any seller researches competitors. We fix what is off and show the date.

Goal

Real seller data

When 20 or more sellers in a category share their numbers through the Health Check, that category switches to real averages, with the number of sellers shown.

What each label means

You will see one of these labels next to the prices for every category in the Builder, the Health Check and Category Intelligence.

Guidance

Suggested starting points written as guidance. Not yet verified against live gigs or seller data. Always compare with the top gigs in your own category.

Spot-checked

A person compared the numbers with live Fiverr gigs by hand on the date shown, and corrected anything that was clearly off.

Early seller data

Based on numbers shared by fewer than 20 sellers in the category. Useful, but still a small sample.

Seller data

Based on numbers shared anonymously by 20 or more sellers in the category. We use the median (the middle value), so one very cheap or very expensive gig cannot skew it.

Where today's numbers come from

The current figures were written by the GigHealthCheck team as guidance for Level 1 and Level 2 sellers: typical package structures, delivery times, paid extras and price steps for each subcategory. They were not measured from live Fiverr listings, so treat them as a starting point.

Keywords and tags are common phrases buyers use to search for each service. Fiverr does not publish search volumes or rankings, so we do not show any. The Gig Builder adjusts prices for your seller level: about 20% lower for new sellers and about 30% higher for established ones.

How the numbers get more accurate

1. Sellers share their numbers anonymously

In the Health Check you can tick "Share my gig's numbers anonymously". We receive only the category and numbers: your three prices, delivery days, revisions, rating, review count, number of gallery images, whether you have a video, response time, number of extras, number of tags and your score. Never your title, description or tags. Sharing is off by default.

2. Spot-checks by hand

Each month we pick a few categories, look at the first page of Fiverr results for the main keyword, note the package prices of Level 1 and Level 2 gigs, and correct our numbers when they are clearly off. This is ordinary research by a person, not an automated tool.

3. "Is this price right?" answers

Every category has a quick way to tell us the prices look too low, too high or about right, with your own Basic price if you want to add it. Only the category and your answer are sent.

What we never do

  • Scrape or crawl Fiverr. It breaks Fiverr's rules, and Fiverr actively blocks automated tools.
  • Ask for your Fiverr login or password.
  • Store your gig title, description or tags. They are checked in your browser.
  • Sell the numbers sellers share with us.
  • Show made-up statistics, fake user counts or fake reviews.
